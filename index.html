From Ground Up

Two years of steel, concrete and daylight, compressed into a single scroll. Keep going to raise it — scroll back to take it down.

Scroll

Phase 01

Foundation

Before anything rises, everything goes down. Forty thousand cubic metres of earth moved, the water table mapped and drained, then piled rafts driven to bedrock. The engineering that decides whether a building stands for fifty years or five is all finished before the first floor plate is poured.

Phase 02

Structure

The frame goes up on a seven-day floor cycle. Post-tensioned concrete cores climb ahead of the steel, the tower crane leapfrogs its own mast, and each slab is surveyed to millimetre tolerance before the next one lands on top of it. This is the fastest, loudest, most visible part of the build.

Phase 03

Finish

Once the envelope is sealed the work turns quiet and precise. Unitised glazing and stone rainscreen are hung panel by panel, services are commissioned floor by floor, and the interiors are fitted out behind the facade. The building stops being a site and starts being an address.
